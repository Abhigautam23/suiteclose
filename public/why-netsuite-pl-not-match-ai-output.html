Why your NetSuite P&L doesn't match AI output

When the P&L you pull from NetSuite doesn't agree with the number your AI assistant returns, there's almost always a data mapping or timing issue behind it — not a model error.

Different sources, different answers

The NetSuite income statement is a report with built-in logic: posting transactions only, grouped by accounting period, in the subsidiary and currency context you selected. An AI tool usually sees none of that logic. It sees an export, an API extract or a warehouse table, and it builds its own answer from the rows it finds.

If the two disagree, the first question is not "which is right?" but "what is each one actually summing?". In almost every case they are summing different things.

Chart of accounts mapping gaps

P&L lines such as "Gross margin" or "Operating expenses" are groupings of GL accounts. NetSuite applies your financial report layout; an AI tool has to infer it. New accounts added since the mapping was last updated, accounts reclassified mid-year, or inactive accounts that still hold historical balances all cause the two to drift.

The fix is a maintained mapping table, owned by finance and enforced in code, that every tool uses — including the AI.

Timing, cut-offs, and period locks

NetSuite assigns each posting to an accounting period, which can differ from the transaction date. Journals posted after an extract was taken, adjustments made after a period was reopened, and revaluation or amortisation entries generated at month-end will all appear in NetSuite but not in a stale copy of the data.

Knowing when your data was last refreshed, and whether the period it covers is closed, is as important as the number itself.

Building a single source of truth

The durable fix is to stop letting each tool build its own P&L. Model the general ledger once, apply your report layout as a mapping, reconcile the result to NetSuite's own trial balance every refresh, and point reports and AI tools at that model. When a number moves, there is one place to look and a test that tells you why.
