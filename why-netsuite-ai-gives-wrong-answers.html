Why NetSuite AI gives wrong answers

If NetSuite AI keeps returning answers that don't match your actual numbers, the AI isn't the problem. The data underneath it is.

The AI isn't broken — the data is

An AI assistant answers questions by querying whatever data it is pointed at. If that data has three slightly different definitions of revenue, the assistant will pick one, and it may not be the one your board pack uses. The model is doing exactly what it was asked; the question was simply ambiguous at the data level.

That is why the same question can return different answers on different days, or from different tools. Nothing about the model changed. The underlying tables, filters and mappings did.

Where the inconsistencies come from

In NetSuite environments the usual culprits are:

  • Posting vs non-posting records. Sales orders, estimates and unapproved journals sit alongside posted transactions. A query that doesn't filter on posting status will happily count them.
  • Transaction date vs accounting period. Your P&L is built by accounting period. Raw exports are often filtered by transaction date, which moves anything posted into a different period.
  • Header vs line amounts. Summing both the main line and the item lines of a transaction double-counts it.
  • Currency and subsidiary context. Foreign-currency amounts, base-currency amounts and consolidated amounts are different numbers. So are figures with and without intercompany eliminations.
  • Undocumented account mappings. If "marketing spend" lives in someone's head rather than in code, every tool reinvents it.

What a clean NetSuite data layer looks like

A clean data layer sits between NetSuite and everything that reads from it — reports, spreadsheets and AI tools. It extracts the raw records on a schedule, applies the accounting rules once (posting status, periods, currency, eliminations, account mappings), and publishes a small set of tested, documented tables: a general ledger fact table, a trial balance, and the agreed metrics on top.

Crucially, it reconciles itself. Every refresh checks that the modelled trial balance ties to NetSuite's own trial balance by period and subsidiary. If it doesn't, the pipeline flags it before anyone — human or AI — reads a wrong number.
