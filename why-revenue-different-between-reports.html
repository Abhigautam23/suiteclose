Why revenue shows different figures between NetSuite reports

Pulling revenue from three different NetSuite reports and getting three different numbers is more common than it should be — and almost always fixable at the data layer.

Where reports drift apart

Three reports, three revenue figures. Typical reasons in NetSuite:

  • One report uses saved-search criteria on transaction type (invoices and cash sales) while the income statement uses posted GL lines on income accounts.
  • One includes credit memos, discounts or shipping lines; another doesn't.
  • One filters by transaction date, another by accounting period.
  • One shows foreign-currency amounts, another base or consolidated currency.
  • A sales or bookings report counts sales orders, which are non-posting and are not revenue.

None of these reports is "wrong" — they answer different questions under the same label.

Revenue recognition rules vs raw GL

If you use revenue recognition or Advanced Revenue Management, invoiced amounts often post to deferred revenue first and are released to income by recognition journals over time. A report built on invoices shows billings; the income statement shows recognised revenue. Both are useful, but they should never share a name.

Subsidiary and consolidation gaps

In NetSuite OneWorld, the subsidiary you run a report from changes the answer: which entities are included, which currency figures are shown in, and whether intercompany sales are eliminated. Revenue at a child subsidiary plus revenue at the parent will not equal consolidated revenue if there is intercompany trading.

Building a canonical revenue model

Agree the definitions first — billings, recognised revenue, net revenue — and write them down. Then build one model per definition from posted GL lines, with explicit rules for credit memos, currency and eliminations, and reconcile recognised revenue to the income statement each period. Every report, spreadsheet and AI tool then reads from those models, so "revenue" means the same thing everywhere.
