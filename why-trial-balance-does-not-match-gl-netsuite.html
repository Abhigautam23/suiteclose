Why your trial balance doesn't match the GL in NetSuite

A trial balance that doesn't tie to the GL is one of the most stressful month-end discoveries — and one of the most common NetSuite data layer failures.

Common causes of TB / GL drift

NetSuite's own trial balance and general ledger come from the same posting lines, so a genuine mismatch between them is rare. What usually doesn't match is a copy of one of them — a saved search, an export or a warehouse table — compared with the other. The common causes:

  • Different subsidiary or consolidation context between the two.
  • Date range vs accounting period filters.
  • Non-posting or unapproved journals included in the search.
  • Foreign-currency amounts compared with base-currency amounts.
  • Different accounting books, if you use multi-book accounting.
  • A stale extract that misses postings made after it was taken.

Intercompany and elimination errors

Intercompany balances should net to zero on consolidation. When they don't — because one side posted in a different period, at a different rate, or to an unmapped account — the elimination leaves a residual. The consolidated TB still balances, but individual accounts look wrong, and anyone rebuilding the TB from raw lines without the elimination entries will get a different answer.

Closed-period manual journals

NetSuite lets users with the right permission post into a locked or closed period, or reopen a period to correct it. Each time that happens, the TB for a period you already reported changes. If nobody is told, the board pack, last month's exports and any AI tool trained on old data quietly disagree with NetSuite from then on.

Automated TB rebuild and validation

The reliable approach is to rebuild the TB from posted GL lines in a modelled data layer, then test it against NetSuite's own TB for every period and subsidiary on every refresh. Add a check that flags any posting dated in a closed period after close. Differences become alerts with the exact account and period, not a month-end investigation.
